- [ ] **The CircuitPython canvas holds a 16-bit frame, 115,200 B of a Pico W's 174 KB heap, and `ulab` may buy the 8-bit frame back.**  Decision 0126 stores colors rather than indexes on CircuitPython because the firmware has no C-speed palette expansion outside displayio's pipeline, which leaves about 41 KB after the panel, font, and canvas on a Pico W: too little for a screen beside wifi and MQTT, and the reason the font example has to import its modules after the panel.  The Pico W build is a full build, so it carries `ulab`, and `ulab` assigns through a boolean mask in C: with an 8-bit `displayio.Bitmap` frame (57,600 B) and a 16-bit strip buffer, `strip16[strip8 == index] = color` per assigned palette entry expands a 6-row strip through a palette of a dozen colors in a dozen C passes over 1,440 pixels, and `set_color` would recolor drawn pixels on CircuitPython as it does on MicroPython.  Bench `np.frombuffer` views over both buffers and the per-strip cost against the 5 ms tick on the Pico W before touching 0126; if it holds, the CircuitPython backend drops to MicroPython's RAM and the import-order rule in the fonts guide goes away.

- [ ] **`Font` draws on the indexed canvas only; the mono OLED and the 16-bit frame need a palette in their own format.**  On MicroPython `Font` blits through a two-entry `GS8` palette, which is right for `GC9A01AIndexed.frame` and wrong for `SSD1306.frame` (`MONO_VLSB`) and `GC9A01A.frame` (`RGB565`); nothing on a `framebuf.FrameBuffer` reports its format, so the panel or the caller has to say. - [ ] **charlcd writes a row in 68 one-byte transactions and nothing paces it.**  A full 16-cell row is about 14 ms of wire time at 100 kHz against the 5 ms tick, and the guide now says so.
